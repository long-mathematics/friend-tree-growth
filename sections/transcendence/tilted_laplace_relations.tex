\section{A fixed-discount derivative ratio recovering the exponent}
\label{tl:section}

Let $F_2(t)=\mathbb E_{\rm edge}C(t)$, and retain the established
$F_2(t)=\exp(\mu t+o(t))$.  For any fixed real $s>\mu$, define
\[
 Z_m(s)=\int_0^\infty t^m e^{-st}F_2(t)\,dt,
 \qquad B_m(s)=\frac{Z_m(s)}{m!}\qquad(m\ge0).
\]
These quantities are finite and strictly positive.  They are the
derivatives of the full-tree transform:
$Z_m(s)=(-1)^m\mathcal M^{(m)}(s)$.

\begin{theorem}[A ratio limit at a fixed subcritical discount]
\label{tl:ratio}
For every fixed $s>\mu$,
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \frac{B_m(s)}{B_{m+1}(s)}\longrightarrow s-\mu,
 \qquad
 \mu=s-\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{B_m(s)}{B_{m+1}(s)}.}
\]
In particular this is a ratio limit for derivatives at one fixed
discount, without assuming convergence of the generation-coefficient
ratios or a power-law amplitude for the core count.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $d=s-\mu>0$, and let $\nu_m$ have density
$t^m e^{-st}F_2(t)/Z_m(s)$ on $(0,\infty)$.  We show
\[
 \int\frac{t}{m}\,d\nu_m(t)\longrightarrow\frac1d.
\]
For every $0<\epsilon<d/3$ there are $T_\epsilon<\infty$ and
$A_\epsilon<\infty$ such that
\[
 F_2(t)\ge e^{(\mu-\epsilon)t}\quad(t\ge T_\epsilon),
 \qquad F_2(t)\le A_\epsilon e^{(\mu+\epsilon)t}
 \quad(t\ge0).
\]
The upper bound extends to all $t$ by continuity and the finite-time
Yule bound.  Thus $Z_m(s)$ is at least
\[
 \int_{T_\epsilon}^\infty t^m e^{-(d+\epsilon)t}\,dt
 =\frac{m!}{(d+\epsilon)^{m+1}}(1-o(1)).
\]

Fix $0<a<1/d<b$.  The upper estimates for the integrals over
$t\le am$ and $t\ge bm$ are gamma-tail estimates with rate
$d-\epsilon$ and shape $m+1$.  Their exponential rate, relative to
the full gamma integral, is $-I((d-\epsilon)a)$ or
$-I((d-\epsilon)b)$, respectively, where
$I(x)=x-1-\log x>0$ for $x\ne1$.
These estimates follow directly by applying the exponential Markov
inequality to a gamma variable, whose moment generating function is
$(1-\theta)^{-m-1}$, and optimizing $\theta$.
For sufficiently small $\epsilon$, both rates dominate the factor
$((d+\epsilon)/(d-\epsilon))^{m+1}$ arising from the lower bound
for $Z_m(s)$.  Consequently
\[
 \nu_m\{t/m\notin[a,b]\}\longrightarrow0
\]
exponentially in $m$ for these fixed $a,b$.

The same argument bounds the first moment of the upper tail: inserting
$t/m$ replaces the upper gamma integral by one of shape $m+2$, with
prefactor $(m+1)/(m(d-\epsilon))$ and the same limiting exponential
rate.  Thus
$\int_{t\ge bm}(t/m)\,d\nu_m\to0$.  The lower tail contribution
is bounded by $a\nu_m(t\le am)$.  On the remaining interval the
variable $t/m$ lies in $[a,b]$.  Letting $a\uparrow1/d$ and
$b\downarrow1/d$ proves the asserted first-moment limit.  Finally,
\[
 \frac{B_m}{B_{m+1}}=
 \frac{(m+1)Z_m}{Z_{m+1}}=
 \frac{1+1/m}{\int(t/m)\,d\nu_m}\longrightarrow d.
\]
\end{proof}

The constants in this concentration argument depend on the error in
$\log F_2(t)=\mu t+o(t)$.  No effective error bound in $m$, monotonicity
of the displayed ratios, or one-sided enclosure of $\mu$ by an individual
ratio is asserted.

\subsection{Positive rational coefficient formulas and explicit finite tails}

Write $a_n=\mathbb E C_n$, and put
\[
 w_n(s)=\frac{(n-1)!}{\prod_{j=2}^n(s+j)},\qquad
 \mathbf x_n(s)=\left(\frac1{s+2},\ldots,\frac1{s+n}\right).
\]
For a finite vector $\mathbf x$, let $H_m(\mathbf x)$ be its complete
homogeneous polynomial, with $H_0=1$.  Equivalently,
$\prod_{x\in\mathbf x}(1-xz)^{-1}=\sum_{m\ge0}H_m(\mathbf x)z^m$.
The full beta series gives
\begin{equation}\label{tl:beta-jet}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 B_m(s)=\sum_{n\ge2}a_nw_n(s)H_m(\mathbf x_n(s)).}
\end{equation}
Every summand is nonnegative and is rational when $s$ is rational.
Indeed, expand the finite product $w_n(s-z)$ in powers of $z$;
equivalently differentiate its beta integral.  Tonelli identifies its
$m$th coefficient with the nonnegative integral $Z_m/m!$ above.
The coefficient computation needs only
\[
 H_m(x_1,\ldots,x_j)
 =H_m(x_1,\ldots,x_{j-1})+x_jH_{m-1}(x_1,\ldots,x_j),
 \qquad w_{n+1}=\frac{n}{s+n+1}w_n.
\]

For a completely explicit finite error bound, take $s>1$, for example
the fixed rational discount $s=2$.  The elementary $a_n\le n-2$
is then sufficient.  If the sum in~\eqref{tl:beta-jet} is stopped at
$n=N\ge2$, its omitted part is at most
\begin{align}
 U_{m,N}(s)=Nw_N(s)\bigg[&
 \frac{N-1}{s-1}
 H_m\!\left(\mathbf x_N(s),\frac1{s-1}\right)\notag\\
 &+\frac{2}{s(s-1)}
 H_m\!\left(\mathbf x_N(s),\frac1s,\frac1{s-1}\right)
 \bigg].\label{tl:rational-tail}
\end{align}
This is a positive rational bound at rational $s>1$, and for each
fixed $s,m$ it tends to zero as
$O_{s,m}(N^{1-s}(\log N)^m)$.

To prove it, first telescope
\[
 T_N(s):=\sum_{n>N}(n-2)w_n(s)
 =Nw_N(s)\left[\frac{N-1}{s-1}+\frac2{s(s-1)}\right].
\]
For example the difference of the right sides at $N$ and $N+1$ is
$(N-1)w_{N+1}$, and the right side tends to zero.  The coefficient
of $z^m$ in $T_N(s-z)$ is exactly~\eqref{tl:rational-tail}.
All underlying terms are positive; coefficientwise domination by
$a_n\le n-2$ proves the bound.  Finally $w_N(s)=O_s(N^{-s-1})$
and $H_m(\mathbf x)\le(\sum x)^m$ prove the stated tail order.

Thus every fixed derivative ratio can be enclosed by rational finite
calculations at $s=2$, with no exact-pressure oracle.  These enclosures
are enclosures of the finite-index ratio itself, not certified brackets
for $\mu$: the remaining limit in $m$ has no established effective
rate.  This is an analytic characterization, not an irrationality test.

\paragraph{Which moment positivity is available.}
The unnormalized moments $Z_m(s)$ have strictly positive Hankel matrices:
for every nonzero real polynomial $p$,
\[
 \sum_{i,j}p_ip_j Z_{i+j}(s)
 =\int_0^\infty p(t)^2e^{-st}F_2(t)\,dt>0.
\]
Here $F_2(t)>0$ for $t>0$.  This assertion concerns the time-tilted
moments $Z_m$, not the factorially normalized $B_m$, not the generator
moments $(\mathcal L^mC)(T_2)$, and not a spectral measure for $F_2'$.
The integral values are not known to be rational, so their positivity
does not produce a rational continued-fraction irrationality proof.
